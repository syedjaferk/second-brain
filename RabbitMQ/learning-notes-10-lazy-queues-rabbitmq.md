<p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Are Lazy Queues?</strong></h2><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Lazy Queues are designed to store messages <strong>primarily on disk</strong> rather than in memory.</li><li>They are optimized for use cases involving <strong>large message backlogs</strong> where minimizing memory usage is critical.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Key Characteristics</strong></h2><ol class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Disk-Based Storage</strong> – Messages are stored on disk immediately upon arrival, rather than being held in memory.</li><li><strong>Low Memory Usage</strong> – Only minimal metadata for messages is kept in memory.</li><li><strong>Scalability</strong> – Can handle <strong>millions of messages</strong> without consuming significant memory.</li><li><strong>Message Retrieval</strong> – Retrieving messages is slower because messages are fetched from disk.</li><li><strong>Durability</strong> – Messages persist on disk, reducing the risk of data loss during RabbitMQ restarts.</li></ol><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Trade-offs</strong></h2><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Latency</strong>: Fetching messages from disk is slower than retrieving them from memory.</li><li><strong>Throughput</strong>: Not suitable for high-throughput, low-latency applications.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Choose Lazy Queues if</h2><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>You need to handle <strong>very large backlogs</strong> of messages.</li><li>Memory is a constraint in your system.Latency and throughput are less critical.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Implementation</h2><h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Pre-requisites</strong></h3><p class="wp-block-paragraph">1.
