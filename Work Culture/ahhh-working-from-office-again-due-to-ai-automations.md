<p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">For the last few years, working from home became a normal part of life, especially in the IT industry (Side effect of Covid). Employees enjoyed flexibility, and companies believed that work could continue smoothly without everyone being in the same place.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Online meetings, chat tools, and cloud systems made it feel like offices were no longer essential.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">However, this thinking has slowly started to change. Many IT companies are now asking employees to return to the office more frequently. This change is not happening overnight, but it is steady and deliberate. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Instead of full remote work, organisations are moving towards hybrid models where employees are expected to be physically present for a certain number of days or hours every week.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Another reason is the impact remote work has had on collaboration. While work continued during the WFH period, many organisations noticed that teamwork was not as effective as before. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Informal discussions, quick problem solving, mentoring of juniors, and spontaneous idea sharing reduced significantly. Over time, this affected both efficiency and team bonding.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Productivity has also become a growing concern. Businesses are currently operating in a difficult global environment with slow growth, high costs, and uncertain markets. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">When margins are tight, companies look closely at how time is being used and how output can be improved. Being in the office gives managers more visibility and control over how work progresses.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Accountability is another factor driving this change. In some cases, flexible work arrangements were misused, with employees logging in briefly or showing activity without meaningful output. To address this, companies have started tracking attendance, work hours, and system usage more closely, both in offices and through digital tools.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">At the same time, organisations are aware that employees value flexibility and work life balance. For this reason, most companies are not forcing an immediate full return to office. Instead, they are gradually tightening rules, introducing minimum office days, and slowly increasing expectations around physical presence.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">This shift does not mean that work from home is disappearing completely. Rather, it is becoming more structured and conditional. Flexibility is still allowed, but it comes with clearer boundaries and stronger expectations around performance and accountability.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">In the end, the office is no longer seen as just a place to sit and work. It is increasingly viewed as a space that helps teams collaborate better, align faster, and respond more effectively to constant change. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">In today’s uncertain and fast moving world, many companies believe that being together, at least some of the time, makes a real difference.</p>
