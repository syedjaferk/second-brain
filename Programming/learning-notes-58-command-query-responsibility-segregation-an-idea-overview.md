<p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Today, i came across a video on ByteMonk on Event Sourcing. In that video, they mentioned about CQRS, then i delved into that. This blog is on understanding CQRS from a high level. I am planning to dive deep into Event Driven Architecture conceptually in upcoming weekend. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">In this blog, i jot down notes for basic understanding of CQRS. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">In the world of software development, there are countless patterns and practices aimed at solving specific problems. One such pattern is CQRS, short for <strong>Command Query Responsibility Segregation</strong>. While it might sound complex (it did for me), the idea is quite straightforward when broken down into simple terms.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What is CQRS?</h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Imagine you run a small bookstore. Customers interact with your store in two main ways</p><ol class="wp-block-list" start="1"><li><strong>They buy books.</strong></li><li><strong>They ask for information about books.</strong></li></ol><p class="wp-block-paragraph">These two activities buying (command) and asking (querying) are fundamentally different. <span style="text-decoration: underline"><em><strong>Buying</strong></em></span> a book <strong><span style="text-decoration: underline"><em>changes</em></span></strong> something in your store (your inventory decreases), whereas <strong><em><span style="text-decoration: underline">asking</span></em></strong> for information <span style="text-decoration: underline"><em><strong>doesn’t change</strong></em></span> anything; it just retrieves details.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">CQRS applies the same principle to software. It separates the operations that <strong>change data</strong> (called commands) from those that <strong>read data</strong> (called queries). This separation brings clarity and efficiency (not sure yet 🙂 )</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">In simpler terms, </p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Commands</strong> are actions like “Add this book to the inventory” or “Update the price of this book.” These modify the state of your system.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Queries</strong> are questions like “How many books are in stock?” or “What’s the price of this book?” These fetch data but don’t alter it.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">By keeping these two types of operations separate, you make your system easier to manage and scale.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why Should You Care About CQRS?</h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Let’s revisit our bookstore analogy. Imagine if every time someone asked for information about a book, your staff had to dig through boxes in the storage room. It would be slow and inefficient!</p><figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2804" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="353" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-79.png?resize=943%2C353&amp;ssl=1" width="943"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Instead, you might keep a catalog at the front desk that’s easy to browse.</p><figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2806" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="353" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-80.png?resize=943%2C353&amp;ssl=1" width="943"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">In software, this means that, </p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Better Performance:</strong> By separating commands and queries, you can optimize them individually. For instance, you can have a simple, fast database for queries and a robust, detailed database for commands.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Simpler Code:</strong> Each part of your system does one thing, making it easier to understand and maintain.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Flexibility:</strong> You can scale the command and query sides independently. If you get a lot of read requests but fewer writes, you can optimize the query side without touching the command side.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">CQRS in Action</h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Let’s say you’re building an app for managing a library. Here’s how CQRS might look,</p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Command:</strong> A librarian adds a new book to the catalog or updates the details of an existing book.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Query:</strong> A user searches for books by title or checks the availability of a specific book.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">The app could use one database to handle commands (storing all the book details and history) and another optimized database to handle queries (focused on quickly retrieving book information).</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Does CQRS Always Make Sense?</h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">As of now, its making items complicated for small applications. As usual every pattern is devised for their niche problems. Single Bolt can go through all Nuts. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">In upcoming blogs, let’s learn more on CQRS. </p>
