<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In DB, for unique identifiers we usually use UUID (Universally Unique Identifier) and ULID (Universally Unique Lexicographically Sortable Identifier).</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">There is some debate going on between ULID and UUID on db performance. Today we are going to experiment this and will provide the facts. </p><h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-tldr">TL;DR</h3><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>CODE:</strong><a href="https://github.com/syedjaferk/benchmarking_uuid_vs_ulid" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://github.com/syedjaferk/benchmarking_uuid_vs_ulid</a></p><figure class="wp-block-image"><img alt="" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" src="https://i0.wp.com/cdn.hashnode.com/res/hashnode/image/upload/v1717402056200/19d802a3-87a3-4a82-8863-beddd1a47d7f.png?ssl=1"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph">There was no big difference between ULID and UUID on indexing runs.</p><ol class="wp-block-list"><li>ULID ids performed <strong>good on reads</strong> compared to UUID.</li><li>Insertion for both UUID and ULID are almost same.</li></ol><h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-uuid">UUID:</h3><p class="wp-block-paragraph">UUIDs are standardized 128-bit identifiers. There are different <a href="https://www.sohamkamani.com/uuid-versions-explained/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">versions</a> of it. In this experiment we use <strong>uuid-4</strong> which is <strong>randomly generated id.</strong> Also this is the most used id in applications.</p><h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-ulid"><a href="https://parottasalna.com/benchmarking-uuid-vs-ulid-for-db-indexing?source=more_series_bottom_blogs#heading-ulid"></a>ULID:</h3><p class="wp-block-paragraph">ULIDs are 128-bit identifiers that are designed to be <strong>sortable</strong> based on time and provide a more compact and readable format. They are composed of two parts:</p><ol class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Timestamp (48 bits)</strong>: Encodes the number of milliseconds since Unix epoch (January 1, 1970).</li><li><strong>Randomness (80 bits)</strong>: Provides uniqueness.</li></ol><p class="wp-block-paragraph">A ULID example looks like this: <code>01HZBFHT49F3NWC2QJVAZE4SS1</code></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">In this benchmarking experiment, we are going to check fact <code>ULID performs better than UUID in DB (under the usecase where are just care about uniqueness)</code></p><h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-stack-used-for-benchmarking">Stack used for benchmarking</h3><ol class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Database:</strong> MongoDB</li><li><strong>Application:</strong> FastAPI</li><li><strong>Performance Testing:</strong> Locust</li></ol><h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-we-are-going-to-check"><a
