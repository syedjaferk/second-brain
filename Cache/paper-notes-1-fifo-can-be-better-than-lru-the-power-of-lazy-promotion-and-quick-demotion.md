<p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Today, i read this paper on cache eviction. Didn’t came directly to this paper, but referred from SIEVE is Simpler than LRU. In this blog, i jot down bullet points that i understood from this paper for future reference. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Historical Context</h2><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>LRU’s Legacy</strong> – LRU (Least Recently Used) has been the foundation of cache eviction algorithms for decades.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Numerous innovations have focused on improving LRU’s <strong>miss ratio</strong> and <strong>throughput</strong> to make it more cache-efficient.</p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>FIFO’s Position</strong> – FIFO (First In, First Out) is simpler and offers <strong>better throughput</strong> and <strong>scalability</strong>, but traditionally lags behind LRU in terms of miss ratio, impacting cache efficiency.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">LRU Algorithm Mechanics</h2><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Core Mechanism:</strong> LRU maintains a <strong>doubly-linked list</strong>, promoting accessed objects to the head of the list (<strong>eager promotion</strong>) and evicting objects at the tail when the cache is full. Relies on <strong>recency</strong> to decide which objects are most valuable.</li><li><strong>Overhead: </strong> Each eager promotion requires updating the linked list structure, involving <strong>at least 6 guard rails</strong>, which increases computational overhead.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">FIFO Algorithm Mechanics</h2><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Core Mechanism:</strong> FIFO evicts the oldest object in the queue, irrespective of its recency or frequency of access. It operates on a simpler structure without maintaining access order.</li></ul><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Overhead:</strong> FIFO avoids the computational cost associated with maintaining metadata or complex data structures, resulting in lower overhead.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Performance Comparison</h2><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>LRU offers better cache efficiency (lower miss ratio) by leveraging recency but suffers from scalability issues due to computational overhead.</li><li>FIFO delivers significantly better throughput and scalability but traditionally lags behind LRU in miss ratio, impacting cache efficiency.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Enhancements to FIFO</h2><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Lazy Promotion (LP):</strong> Performs promotion only at eviction time, avoiding frequent updates and reducing computational complexity.</li></ul><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Quick Demotion (QD):</strong> Quickly removes most objects after insertion using a small probationary FIFO queue, reducing the opportunity cost of waiting for new objects to traverse the entire queue.</li></ul><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>QD-LP-FIFO:</strong> Uses two FIFO queues to cache data and a ghost FIFO queue to track evicted objects, enhancing performance and balancing promotion and demotion strategies.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Enhanced FIFO Algorithms</h2><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>FIFO-Reinsertion &amp; CLOCK:</strong>  FIFO-Reinsertion and CLOCK are approximations of LRU, combining FIFO’s simplicity with aspects of recency. Achieves a lower miss ratio than LRU across diverse workloads while avoiding the computational cost of maintaining a doubly-linked list.</li></ul><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>QD-Enhanced Algorithms:</strong> State-of-the-art algorithms such as ARC and LIRS, when enhanced with Quick Demotion, outperform traditional LRU in terms of scalability and hit rates.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Real-World Use Cases</h2><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Performance-Centric Systems:</strong>  FIFO-based algorithms are preferred in performance-centric systems like networking buffers or distributed systems due to their scalability and reduced overhead.</li></ul><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Workload Diversity</strong>: Lazy Promotion and Quick Demotion make FIFO-based algorithms better suited for diverse access patterns, such as sequential scans or bursty traffic.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Reddit Discussion: <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/programming/comments/1cj2faa/fifo_is_better_than_lru_the_power_of_lazy/">https://www.reddit.com/r/programming/comments/1cj2faa/fifo_is_better_than_lru_the_power_of_lazy/</a></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Authors Blog: <a href="https://blog.jasony.me/system/cache/2023/06/24/fifo-lru">https://blog.jasony.me/system/cache/2023/06/24/fifo-lru</a></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">ACM: <a href="https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3593856.3595887">https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3593856.3595887</a></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Paper Download: <a href="https://sigops.org/s/conferences/hotos/2023/papers/yang.pdf">https://sigops.org/s/conferences/hotos/2023/papers/yang.pdf</a></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p>
