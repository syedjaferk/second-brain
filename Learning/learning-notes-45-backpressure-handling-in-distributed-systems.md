<p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Today, i dive deeeeep in to backpressure handling. This is a continuation of <a href="https://parottasalna.com/2025/01/06/learning-notes-43-avoiding-insurmountable-queue-backlogs-aws-cloud-pattern/">How AWS Solves Backlog Queue ?</a> . Backpressure is one of the mechanism to precaution to handle a backlog queue from occuring. In this blog i jot down notes on backpressure for my futureself. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What is Backpressure?</h2><figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2480" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="489" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-63.png?resize=1151%2C489&amp;ssl=1" width="1151"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Backpressure occurs when a downstream system (consumer) cannot keep up with the rate of data being sent by an upstream system (producer). In distributed systems, this can arise in scenarios such as</p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>A message queue filling up faster than it is drained.</li><li>A database struggling to handle the volume of write requests.</li><li>A streaming system overwhelmed by incoming data.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph">The mismatch between production and consumption rates leads to resource contention, high latency, or data loss, depending on how the system is designed to handle overflow.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Causes of Backpressure</h2><ol class="wp-block-list" start="1"><li><strong>High Volume of Requests</strong>: An upstream service may generate data at a rate higher than the downstream service’s processing capacity.</li><li><strong>Resource Constraints</strong>: Limited CPU, memory, or disk I/O on downstream systems can throttle processing.</li><li><strong>Network Congestion</strong>: Bandwidth limitations or high latency in network communication can exacerbate backpressure.</li><li><strong>Slow Consumers</strong>: Consumers with inefficient logic or prolonged processing times can become bottlenecks.</li><li><strong>Unbalanced Load Distribution</strong>: Improper load balancing across distributed nodes can lead to some nodes becoming overwhelmed.</li></ol><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Symptoms of Backpressure</h2><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Increased Latency</strong>: Requests take longer to process as queues grow.</li><li><strong>System Crashes</strong>: Overloaded components may exhaust resources and fail.</li><li><strong>Data Loss</strong>: Systems without sufficient buffering or overflow handling might lose data.</li><li><strong>Queue Overflows</strong>: Buffers and queues hit their capacity limits.</li><li><strong>Erratic Throughput</strong>: The rate of data processing becomes unstable.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Solutions to Manage Backpressure</h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Addressing backpressure requires strategies at both the architectural and operational levels. Below are effective techniques, </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong><span style="text-decoration: underline"><a href="https://parottasalna.com/2025/01/02/learning-notes-27-rate-limit-requests/">Rate Limiting</a></span></strong></h3><figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2486" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="526" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-65.png?resize=1400%2C526&amp;ssl=1" width="1400"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Rate limiting prevents upstream systems from overwhelming downstream systems by enforcing limits on data transmission. This ensures a sustainable flow of requests or data.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Algorithms like the <strong>Token Bucket Algorithm</strong> allow a certain number of requests to pass through within a specific time frame, replenishing tokens at a steady rate. If the bucket is empty, excess requests are delayed or rejected.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Tools such as API gateways (e.g., NGINX, Kong) implement rate-limiting policies to protect backend systems from overload.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong><span style="text-decoration: underline">Load Shedding</span></strong></h3><figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2490" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="392" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-66.png?resize=684%2C392&amp;ssl=1" width="684"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Load shedding focuses on maintaining system health by sacrificing non-critical operations. For instance, in e-commerce, recommendations can be deprioritized while processing payments remains a priority. <a href="https://parottasalna.com/2024/12/29/learning-notes-17-circuit-breaker-pattern-cloud-pattern/">Circuit breakers</a> (e.g., Netflix’s Hystrix) detect failure conditions and prevent further requests to overloaded services, giving them time to recover. Priority queues are used to ensure high-value or high-priority tasks are processed first, while low-priority requests may be discarded during high load.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Buffering</strong></h3><figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2492" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="497" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-67.png?resize=778%2C497&amp;ssl=1" width="778"/></figure><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Temporarily stores data in memory or on disk to smooth out spikes in load. </li></ul><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Buffering acts as a shock absorber, holding excess data until the system can process it. This is particularly useful in scenarios with bursty traffic patterns.</li><li>Message queues like RabbitMQ, Apache Kafka, or Amazon SQS store incoming data and let consumers process it at their own pace.</li><li>It is crucial to size buffers appropriately to handle peak loads without excessive memory or disk usage.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Elastic Scaling</strong></h3><figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2495" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="376" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-68.png?resize=1054%2C376&amp;ssl=1" width="1054"/></figure><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Elastic scaling involves adding or removing resources based on demand. For example, in cloud environments, auto-scaling policies can increase server instances when traffic spikes and reduce them during low usage.</li><li>Kubernetes Horizontal Pod Autoscaler (HPA) adjusts the number of pods in a cluster based on metrics like CPU usage or custom application-level metrics.</li><li>Serverless platforms (e.g., AWS Lambda) provide fine-grained scaling by executing functions in response to events without the need for pre-provisioned resources.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Monitoring and Alerting</strong></h3><figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2498" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="331" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-69.png?resize=865%2C331&amp;ssl=1" width="865"/></figure><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Detects backpressure issues early for proactive resolution.</li></ul><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Monitoring tools (e.g., Prometheus, Grafana) provide visibility into system health by tracking metrics such as queue lengths, request latency, and throughput.</li><li>Setting thresholds for critical metrics and configuring alerts enables teams to respond quickly to potential issues before they escalate.</li><li>Historical data analysis can reveal trends, aiding in capacity planning and preventing future bottlenecks.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h3 class="wp-block-heading">Practical Example: Backpressure in Apache Kafka</h3><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Apache Kafka is a popular distributed streaming platform that addresses backpressure with</p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Consumer Lag Monitoring</strong>: Tracks how far behind a consumer is from the head of the topic.</li><li><strong>Max Poll Interval</strong>: Ensures consumers process messages within a defined interval, preventing slow consumers from holding up processing.</li><li><strong>Producer Throttling</strong>: Controls the rate of message production based on broker limits.</li><li><strong>Disk-based Buffering</strong>: Uses disk storage to handle spikes in message volume.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Do you have any experiences or favorite tools for handling backpressure? Share them in the comments below!</p>
