<p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Today, i learnt about various types of id generations. ID Generations are a key part in handling data, uniquely identifying entities. My goto ID generation is UUID4. You might have seem some debates on <a href="https://parottasalna.com/2024/06/09/benchmarking-uuid4-vs-ulid-for-db-indexing/">UUID vs ULID for better performance</a>. </p><blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow"><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Related: </strong><a href="https://parottasalna.com/2024/06/09/benchmarking-uuid4-vs-ulid-for-db-indexing/">https://parottasalna.com/2024/06/09/benchmarking-uuid4-vs-ulid-for-db-indexing/</a></p></blockquote><p class="wp-block-paragraph">In this blog i jot down notes on various ID generations, for quick access for my future self. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">In software development, especially for cloud applications, it’s crucial to assign unique identifiers to data records, resources, and entities. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Traditionally, developers (me) have used UUIDs (Universally Unique Identifiers) for this purpose. A typical UUID looks like this: <code>1e7cacb7-af9f-4f07-88fd-45370c25ab62</code>. While UUIDs are widely adopted, they have certain drawbacks, such as large size and lack of natural ordering, which can lead to inefficiencies in performance, storage, and indexing at scale.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">To address these issues, several alternative methods for generating unique identifiers have been developed, each with its own advantages and disadvantages. </p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Here are some of the most notable alternatives,</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>1. Auto-Incrementing IDs (Sequential IDs)</strong></h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Auto-incrementing IDs are numeric values that increase by one with each new record, commonly used in relational databases.</p><figure class="wp-block-image size-large is-resized"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2137" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="554" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-6.png?resize=568%2C554&amp;ssl=1" style="width:340px;height:auto" width="568"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Pros:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Simple to implement and understand.</li><li>Efficient in terms of storage due to smaller numeric data types.</li><li>Easily indexed for better database performance.</li><li>Suitable for small-scale systems where order matters and global uniqueness isn’t required.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Cons:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Not suitable for distributed systems due to potential conflicts.</li><li>Predictable, posing security risks as IDs can be easily guessed.</li><li>Requires database coordination to avoid duplicates in sharded systems.</li></ul><blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow"><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Note: <strong>This alternative is provided as a reference</strong> for the simple implementation that many people learn with… but is <strong><em>most likely</em></strong> not what you want, <strong>it’s normally considered a bad practice unless you have some specialized use case.</strong></p></blockquote><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>2. Snowflake ID (Twitter Snowflake)</strong></h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Snowflake IDs are 64-bit unique identifiers composed of a timestamp, machine ID, and sequence number. They are designed for distributed systems to ensure uniqueness across multiple machines.</p><figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2140" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="304" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-7.png?resize=700%2C304&amp;ssl=1" width="700"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Example: <code><strong>5643574219214851220</strong></code></em></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Pros:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Distributed and scalable, ideal for distributed systems.</li><li>Timestamp component provides approximate ordering.</li><li>Ensures uniqueness across systems.</li><li>Useful for scalable, time-ordered unique IDs, especially in social media or messaging apps.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Cons:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>More complex to implement than simple IDs.</li><li>Timestamp-based IDs can reveal timing information.</li><li>Requires careful configuration of machine identifiers to avoid collisions.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Papers:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><a href="https://pypi.org/project/snowflake-id/">https://pypi.org/project/snowflake-id/</a></li><li><a href="https://www.npmjs.com/package/snowflake-id">https://www.npmjs.com/package/snowflake-id</a></li><li><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake_ID">https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake_ID</a></li><li><a href="https://softwaremind.com/blog/the-unique-features-of-snowflake-id-and-its-comparison-to-uuid/">https://softwaremind.com/blog/the-unique-features-of-snowflake-id-and-its-comparison-to-uuid/</a></li><li><a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04879">https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04879</a></li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>3. KSUID (K-Sortable Unique Identifier)</strong></h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">KSUIDs are 160-bit identifiers that combine a timestamp with a random payload, allowing for lexicographical sorting based on creation time.</p><figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2146" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="399" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-8.png?resize=720%2C399&amp;ssl=1" width="720"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Papers:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>NPM: <a href="https://www.npmjs.com/package/ksuid" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://www.npmjs.com/package/ksuid</a></li><li>PyPI: <a href="https://pypi.org/project/svix-ksuid/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://pypi.org/project/svix-ksuid/</a> or <a href="https://pypi.org/project/ksuid/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://pypi.org/project/ksuid/</a></li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="f3fa"><strong><em>Example</em>: </strong><code><strong>1avvTqCSFGnD5LDc4hN6GFFCAXD</strong></code></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Pros:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Sortable by creation time.</li><li>More human-readable than UUIDs.</li><li>Suitable for high-scale distributed systems.</li><li>Ideal for systems requiring time-ordered unique IDs, such as event logging.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Cons:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Timestamp exposure may not be ideal for privacy.</li><li>Slightly more complex to generate than UUIDs.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>4. ULID (Universally Unique Lexicographically Sortable Identifier)</strong></h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">ULIDs are 128-bit identifiers that combine a timestamp with randomness, enabling lexicographical sorting and ensuring uniqueness.</p><figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2148" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="304" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-9.png?resize=720%2C304&amp;ssl=1" width="720"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Papers:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>NPM: <a href="https://www.npmjs.com/package/ulid" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://www.npmjs.com/package/ulid</a></li><li>PyPI: <a href="https://pypi.org/project/python-ulid/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://pypi.org/project/python-ulid/</a></li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="49af"><strong><em>Example</em>: </strong><code><strong>22H1UECHZX3FGGSZ7A9Y9BVC1</strong></code></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Pros:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Sortable based on creation time.</li><li>More human-readable than UUIDs.</li><li>Suitable for high-scale distributed systems.</li><li>Useful for systems requiring lexicographical sorting along with globally unique identifiers, such as e-commerce or document management systems.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Cons:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Similar to KSUID, the timestamp is exposed, which might not be ideal for privacy.</li><li>Slightly more complex to generate than UUIDs.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>5. NanoID</strong></h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">NanoID is a JavaScript library designed for generating unique IDs that are small, fast, and secure.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Papers &amp; References:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Github: <a href="https://github.com/ai/nanoid">https://github.com/ai/nanoid</a></li><li>NPM: <a href="https://www.npmjs.com/package/nanoid" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://www.npmjs.com/package/nanoid</a></li><li>PyPI: <a href="https://pypi.org/project/nanoid/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://pypi.org/project/nanoid/</a></li></ul><figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2151" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="294" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-10.png?resize=720%2C294&amp;ssl=1" width="720"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="1b88"><strong><em>Example:</em> </strong><code><strong>E9SxJKL8_K5emHi2B-noZ</strong></code></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Pros:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Shorter IDs compared to UUIDs, making them more URL-friendly.</li><li>Faster generation due to smaller size.</li><li>Cryptographically strong random IDs, enhancing security.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Cons:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>May not be suitable for all systems, especially those requiring longer IDs for uniqueness.</li><li>Primarily designed for JavaScript environments, which might limit usage in other ecosystems.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>6. ObjectID (MongoDB ObjectID)</strong></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">ObjectIDs are 12-byte identifiers used by MongoDB, consisting of a timestamp, machine ID, process ID, and a counter.</p><figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2154" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="337" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-11.png?resize=720%2C337&amp;ssl=1" width="720"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Example: </strong><code>102e1b71bcd16cd721434331</code><br/>A 24-character hexadecimal string consisting of a timestamp, machine ID, and process ID.</p><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Pros:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Sortable by creation time.</li><li>Compact size compared to UUIDs.</li><li>Ensures uniqueness within a MongoDB collection.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Cons:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Specific to MongoDB; may not be suitable for other systems.</li><li>Exposes information about the server and time of creation, which might be a privacy concern.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Papers:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><a href="https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/reference/method/ObjectId/">https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/reference/method/ObjectId/</a></li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">7. <strong>CUID (Collision-Resistant Unique Identifier)</strong></p><p class="wp-block-paragraph">CUIDs are designed to be human-readable, URL-safe, and collision-resistant, making them suitable for distributed systems.</p><figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-2157" data-recalc-dims="1" decoding="async" height="343" loading="lazy" src="https://i0.wp.com/parottasalna.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/image-12.png?resize=720%2C343&amp;ssl=1" width="720"/></figure><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Pros:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Human-readable and URL-friendly.</li><li><strong>Collision-resistant</strong>, making it ideal for distributed systems.</li><li>Works well in both small and large-scale applications</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Cons:</em></p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Larger than some alternatives like NanoID.</li><li>Less widely adopted compared to UUIDs and Snowflake IDs.</li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph">Papers &amp; References:</p><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><a href="https://www.npmjs.com/package/@paralleldrive/cuid2">https://www.npmjs.com/package/@paralleldrive/cuid2</a></li></ul><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Choosing the Right Alternative</strong></h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">When deciding which unique identifier to use for your cloud application, consider the following factors:</p><ol class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Scalability:</strong> For distributed systems, prioritize IDs like Snowflake or KSUID, which are designed for scalability.</li><li><strong>Performance:</strong> For faster generation and smaller storage requirements, look into NanoID or auto-incrementing IDs.</li><li><strong>Sorting Needs:</strong> If you need time-ordered identifiers, ULID, KSUID, or Snowflake IDs are great options.</li><li><strong>Readability:</strong> For user-facing systems or URLs, CUIDs or NanoIDs might be more suitable.</li><li><strong>Ecosystem Compatibility:</strong> Some options, like ObjectID, are tied to specific databases (MongoDB), which can be a deciding factor.</li></ol><p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>UUID and Its Different Versions</strong></h2><p class="wp-block-paragraph">UUIDs (Universally Unique Identifiers) are a widely-used standard for generating unique identifiers.
